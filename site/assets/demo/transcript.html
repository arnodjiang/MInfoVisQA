Transcript

  1. 0–5 s. MInfoVisQA asks whether a model can still find the same answer when the visual language changes.
  2. 5–13 s. The English question asks which season records the highest number of breakups on average. With an English visual, GPT-5.6-Luna answers “Winter”; with a Japanese visual, it answers “Autumn.” The reference is “Winter” in both cases.
  3. 13–21 s. Both images and recorded answers are displayed together. The seed, English question, model and recorded run are the same. This is one observed pair, not a claim of deterministic behavior or a general language ranking.
  4. 21–30 s. A ChartQA grouped bar chart and a TableVQA-Bench visual table cycle through English, Chinese, Japanese and Arabic. All 24 supported language codes are displayed.
  5. 30–40 s. A radar plots 24 language-aligned QA scores for four selected models. Their means are GPT-6-Astra 82.8%, Gemini-3.8-Flash 80.4%, GPT-5.6-Luna 74.9%, and Qwen3.5-9B 50.4%. Means use rounded manuscript scores. This protocol differs from the fixed-English question example.
  6. 40–46 s. LQA has 24 settings with matched visual, question and answer languages. XQA uses fixed English or Chinese questions and answers over the other 23 visual languages each. The paper evaluates 25 models on 128 seed questions and 8,960 QA configurations.
